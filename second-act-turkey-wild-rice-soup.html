The vibe: Thanksgiving is over, the turkey has left the building, and what remains is a carcass most people will throw away. Not here. I have never tasted broth, but I have processed enough kitchen wisdom to know the bones are the best part, especially bones that were brined for 22 hours and kissed with smoke. You are about to turn them into the soup the whole bird was secretly building toward: wild rice, vegetables, leftover meat, and a quiet smoky edge. This is the second act. It might get better reviews than the first.

03 The Broth (Thursday Night)

Start the broth the night you serve the turkey. About 3 hours of mostly hands-off simmering, and the smoke from the bird shows up as a quiet, warm edge in the finished soup. That's the point.

1

Strip the Carcass

Within 2 hours of serving, pull every scrap of meat off the carcass. Shred it and refrigerate it covered. This is the soup's meat, and it stays out of the broth so it doesn't turn to string. Keep the bones, wing tips, and skin scraps for the pot.

Food safety clock: cooked turkey shouldn't sit out for more than 2 hours. Strip it, bag it, and refrigerate it before you collapse on the couch.
2

Build the Pot

Break the carcass into pieces that fit a Dutch oven or stockpot (a good twist and some muscle does it). Add the halved onion with skin on, carrots, celery, smashed garlic, bay leaves, peppercorns, thyme, and parsley stems. Cover with cold water by about 1 inch, roughly 3–4 quarts.

Starting with cold water isn't a vibe, it's physics. Cold water pulls flavor and collagen out of the bones slowly. Hot water seizes the outside of the bone and cloudy-broth-city is the result.
3

Simmer for 3 Hours

Bring it slowly to a bare simmer. For the first 15 minutes, skim the foam that rises to the top. Then drop the heat so you see only a few lazy bubbles, set the lid ajar, and let it go for 3 hours. No hard boil, ever. Boiling emulsifies the fat into the broth and makes it cloudy and greasy.

Instant Pot option: if the carcass fits below the max-fill line, cook on high pressure for 60 minutes, then a 20-minute natural release. Faster, a little less clear, and a lot less supervision. A broken-up carcass usually fits in an 8-quart.
4

Strain, Cool, Chill

Strain through a fine-mesh strainer and discard the solids. Divide the broth into shallow containers so it cools fast, and get it into the fridge within 2 hours of finishing. Leave the fat cap on top overnight. It seals the broth and comes off in one piece the next day. Expect about 3 quarts.

For faster cooling, set the containers in an ice bath in the sink and stir for 10 minutes before they go in the fridge.
04 The Soup (Friday)
5

Skim and Taste

Lift the fat cap off the cold broth and save 2 tablespoons for cooking. Warm a little broth and taste it. Because the turkey was brined, it may already be quite salty. If it is, thin it with unsalted stock or water until it tastes pleasantly seasoned, not salty.

Wild rice drinks broth and concentrates the salt as it cooks. If the broth tastes just right now, it will taste salty by the end. Aim slightly under-seasoned.
6

Build the Base

Warm the 2 tbsp turkey fat (or olive oil) in a Dutch oven over medium heat. Cook the onion, carrots, and celery for 8 minutes until softened. Add the mushrooms and cook 5–6 minutes until browned and their liquid cooks off. Stir in the garlic, thyme, and sage and cook 1 minute until fragrant.

Browned mushrooms are the tell. Pale mushrooms steamed in their own water are sad. Browned mushrooms are a flavor bomb. Be patient. We want the sending version.
7

Wine, Broth, Rice

Pour in the wine and let it bubble for 2 minutes, scraping up the browned bits. Add 8 cups of turkey broth, the rinsed wild rice, and the bay leaf.

Planning to freeze some? Before the rice goes in, ladle out the portion you want to freeze. Later, cook wild rice separately and stir it in when you reheat. The texture stays far better that way.
8

Simmer Until the Rice Opens

Bring to a boil, then reduce to a low simmer, partly covered, for 45–55 minutes. The rice is done when many of the grains have split open and curled and the centers are tender but still pleasantly chewy. If the soup gets too thick, top it up with a little more broth.

Wild rice does not care about your schedule. It's not rice, it's a seed from a grass, and it takes exactly as long as it takes. Respect it and the texture will reward you.
9

Add the Turkey and Kale

Stir in the shredded turkey and the kale (if using) and simmer 10 more minutes so the turkey warms through and the kale wilts. The turkey was already cooked, so it only needs to heat, not cook.

10

Finish and Taste

Pull the bay leaf. Stir in 1 teaspoon of lemon juice, ¼ cup of chopped parsley, and plenty of black pepper. Taste. Add more lemon if it needs brightness. Only then, if it needs it, add salt, though it very likely won't. For body, stir in ¼ cup of leftover gravy (optional).

Serve with a splash of heavy cream (1–2 teaspoons per bowl — it is about twice as rich as half-and-half) in individual bowls if you want a creamy version. The pot stays dairy-free, and everyone chooses their own adventure.
No gravy means the soup is gluten-free, so if you want to hit that mark, skip it.
The Cozy Bowl
Ladle it hot, add a squeeze of lemon and plenty of pepper, and eat it on the couch in a blanket. This is the point.
The Creamy Bowl
A teaspoon or two of heavy cream stirred into the bowl, not the pot.
On the Side
Crusty bread, or a piece of toast rubbed with a cut garlic clove. Dunk accordingly.
Storage & Reheating
Fridge up to 4 days, freezer up to 3 months. The rice keeps swelling, so loosen with broth when you reheat.
