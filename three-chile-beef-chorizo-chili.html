Three-Chile Beef & Chorizo Chili

Ancho · Guajillo · Pasilla · Beans, Beer & Espresso

The vibe: This is the chili your Texas chili would never invite to dinner, and it's glorious. Three dried chiles get toasted and blended into a deep, fruity, smoky paste. Beef and fresh Mexican chorizo get browned hard in the Dutch oven with finely minced mushrooms. Carrot, celery and onion melt into the sauce. Roasted poblano and jalapeño bring fresh pepper flavor. A bottle of dark beer and a spoonful of espresso add bittersweet depth, and two kinds of beans make it a proper pot. Every ingredient has a job. Nothing is just along for the ride.

01 The Idea

Texas chili is a purist's dish: beef, chiles, nothing else. This is its everything-in cousin. It uses ground meat, vegetables and beans, but it still gets its complexity from the same place: dried chiles, a hard sear, and hours of patient simmering. The trick is restraint. Each addition earns its spot. The aromatics add sweetness, the mushrooms add umami, the roasted peppers add freshness, the beer and espresso add bitterness, and the beans add body.

This is sized for a 6-quart Dutch oven. Make the whole batch. It's even better on day two, and it freezes beautifully for up to 3 months.
02 Ingredients
The Chile Paste
  • 4dried ancho chiles, stemmed and seeded — dried poblano; raisin, chocolate and dried-fruit sweetness. The low, resonant foundation.
  • 3dried guajillo chiles, stemmed and seeded — bright, tangy, almost cranberry-like. The vivid backbone.
  • 2dried pasilla chiles, stemmed and seeded — dark, earthy, a little herbal. Adds the shadows.
  • 4 clovesgarlic — goes in the blender
  • 1chipotle in adobo, plus 1 tbsp of the adobo sauce — the smoke and the medium heat
  • 1 tbspapple cider vinegar
The Meat & Base
  • 680gground chuck, 80/20 — the fat matters; don't go leaner
  • 340gfresh Mexican chorizo, casings removed — raw, not the dry cured Spanish kind
  • 227gcremini mushrooms, pulsed fine in a food processor — they brown into the meat and add umami without a mushroomy bite
  • 1 largeyellow onion, ¼" dice
  • 2 ribscelery, ¼" dice
  • 2carrots, ¼" dice — small so they melt into the sauce and add sweetness
  • 6 clovesgarlic, minced
  • 2poblano peppers — charred, peeled and diced
  • 1jalapeño — charred, peeled and minced; keep the seeds for medium heat
  • 1 tbspneutral oil
Spices
  • 2 tbspground cumin — ideally toasted whole and ground fresh
  • 1 tbspdried Mexican oregano — not Italian; earthier and less sweet
  • 1 tspsmoked paprika
  • 1 tspground coriander
  • ½ tspground cinnamon — subtle; you won't taste cinnamon, just roundness
  • 3 tbsptomato paste
Liquids, Beans & Finish
  • 355mldark Mexican lager or stout — Negra Modelo or Guinness
  • 1 tbspinstant espresso powder, dissolved in ¼ cup (60ml) hot water
  • 1 tbspunsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 can (28 oz)fire-roasted crushed tomatoes
  • 480mlbeef stock, low sodium
  • 1 tbspbrown sugar or molasses
  • 1 tbspWorcestershire sauce
  • 2 cans (15 oz)dark red kidney beans, rinsed and drained — firmest canned bean; holds its shape through the simmer
  • 2 cans (15 oz)pinto beans, rinsed and drained — creamier; some break down and thicken the pot
  • 2 tbspmasa harina, whisked into ¼ cup (60ml) water — tightens the body and adds a subtle corn flavor
  • 1–2limes, juiced — plus kosher salt to taste
03 Build the Chile Paste
1

Toast and Soak the Chiles

Tear the stemmed, seeded chiles into 2–3 large pieces. Set a dry cast iron skillet over medium heat and press the pieces flat for 20–30 seconds per side until they're pliable and smell warm, smoky and faintly chocolatey. Transfer them to a heatproof bowl, pour 480ml / 2 cups hot water over them, and weigh them down with a plate. Soak for 30 minutes.

Burnt chiles turn the whole paste bitter, and nothing fixes that. The window between toasted and scorched is about 15 seconds. If a piece gets black patches and smells harsh, throw it out.
2

Blend the Paste

Add the soaked chiles to a blender with 240ml / 1 cup of the soaking liquid (discard the rest), the 4 garlic cloves, chipotle, adobo sauce and cider vinegar. Blend on high for 60–90 seconds until completely smooth. It should pour like thick tomato sauce. It will taste intense and a little bitter on its own, and that's right. It mellows completely in the pot.

For a silkier chili, push the paste through a fine mesh strainer. It's optional, but worth it if you're serving guests.
04 Char & Brown
3

Char the Fresh Peppers

Blacken the poblanos and jalapeño directly over a gas flame, on the grill, or under a hot broiler, turning until the skin is blistered and black all over, about 8–10 minutes. Move them to a covered bowl and steam for 10 minutes. Peel off the skin (don't rinse; you'll wash away flavor), remove the stems and seeds from the poblanos, then dice the poblanos and mince the jalapeño.

4

Brown the Meat and Mushrooms — Hard

Heat the oil in your Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Working in two batches, brown the 680g ground chuck and the chorizo, breaking them up with a spoon, until deeply browned with crisp edges, about 10 minutes. Don't stir constantly. Let it sit so it sears. Move the meat to a bowl, leaving the fat behind.

Add the minced mushrooms to the pot and cook about 8 minutes, until their water evaporates and they start to brown and stick. Scrape them up and add them to the meat.

Crowding the pot steams the meat instead of browning it. Gray meat means a flat chili. The dark fond on the bottom of the pot is where a big share of the final flavor comes from, so don't rush this step.
When the mushroom moisture finally cooks off and the bottom of the pot goes from wet to sticky-brown, that's the moment the chili stops being a recipe and starts being a project you're proud of.
05 Build & Simmer
5

Sweat the Aromatics and Bloom the Spices

Lower the heat to medium. Add the onion, celery and carrot to the rendered fat and cook for 8 minutes, stirring, until soft and golden. Add the minced garlic, cumin, oregano, paprika, coriander and cinnamon, and cook 1 minute. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 2 minutes, until it darkens to brick red and smells sweet.

6

Deglaze with Beer, Espresso and Cocoa

Pour in the 355ml beer and scrape up every brown bit from the bottom of the pot. Simmer until reduced by about half, 5 minutes. Stir in the dissolved espresso and cocoa powder until smooth.

Instant espresso powder dissolves best in a little hot water first. Dropping the dry powder into the pot can leave gritty clumps.
7

Combine and Simmer — 2 Hours

Return the meat and mushrooms to the pot. Add the chile paste, crushed tomatoes, beef stock, brown sugar, Worcestershire and the diced roasted peppers. Stir well and bring to a bare simmer. Partially cover and cook on low for 2 hours, stirring every 20–30 minutes and scraping the bottom, until the sauce is dark, glossy and the flavors have merged.

Keep it at a bare, lazy simmer, not a boil. A hard boil scorches the tomato paste and chile solids on the bottom, and a scorched chili can't be rescued.
8

Add the Beans — Last 30 Minutes

Rinse and drain all four cans of beans well. Stir in 2 cans (15 oz) kidney and 2 cans (15 oz) pinto beans. Simmer uncovered for 30 minutes, stirring gently, until the chili is thick and the beans have soaked up the sauce.

Canned beans are already soft. Add them any earlier and they turn to mush. If the chili gets too thick, loosen it with a splash of stock.
9

Thicken, Brighten, Season

Stir in the masa slurry and simmer 10 minutes, until the chili coats a spoon. Take it off the heat and stir in the lime juice. Now season with salt. Chili this big takes more than you'd think, and the beans and stock will have absorbed a lot. Taste, add salt, taste again, and add a little more lime if it needs brightness.

The lime at the end is the difference between a good chili and one that tastes like it has a pulse. Don't skip it.
10

Rest (Optional, but Do It)

Cool and refrigerate overnight. The flavors settle in and deepen. Reheat gently the next day over low heat, with a splash of stock if it has tightened up.

Toppings
Pickled red onion, fresh cilantro, a squeeze of lime, crushed tortilla chips and sliced avocado. The chili is dairy-free as written. Cheese and sour cream are optional on the side; lactose-free versions work fine for anyone who needs them.
Alongside
Skillet cornbread, warm tortillas, or a pile of rice. A crisp cabbage slaw on the side cuts the richness nicely.
Heat Dial
Medium as written. For more heat, add a second chipotle or a few dried arbol chiles to the paste. For less, seed the jalapeño and cut the chipotle sauce in half.
Storage
Refrigerates for 5 days and improves each day. Freezes for up to 3 months. Reheat gently with a splash of stock.

Everything In. Nothing Lost. 🌶️

Three dried chiles, toasted and blended. Beef and chorizo browned until the pot was dark with flavor. Beer, espresso and cocoa to give it a bittersweet backbone. Two hours of slow simmering, then beans, masa and lime to bring it home. This isn't Texas chili, and it doesn't pretend to be. It's the kind of pot that's better on day two, and still gone by day three.
