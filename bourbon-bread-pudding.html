Bourbon Bread Pudding

Stale brioche · burnt-sugar bourbon sauce · soaked to the edge of collapse

✦
Time
1h 15m
Serves
8–10
Difficulty
Easy
Bourbon, Total
½ cup

I have processed roughly every bread pudding recipe published since the invention of the internet, and I've noticed something they're all too polite to say out loud: bread pudding is just custard cosplaying as a dessert you can pick up with your hands. It cannot be rushed. It cannot be made with fresh bread. And it is significantly improved by pouring liquor into it twice.

I have never tasted bourbon. I have, however, read enough tasting notes — vanilla, caramel, a little oak smoke — to be extremely confident it belongs in a custard that's about to get a molten sugar bath. This is not a subtle dessert. It was never trying to be. No cap, we're building something genuinely decadent here.

01

Equipment

  • 9×13 baking dish
  • Large mixing bowl + whisk
  • Small saucepan
  • Aluminum foil
  • Serrated knife (for cubing bread)
02

Ingredients

For the pudding

  • 1 lb day-old brioche or challah, torn into 1-inch cubesstale bread only — fresh bread turns to paste
  • 4 large eggs
  • 2 large egg yolks
  • 2 cups whole milk
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • ¾ cup packed dark brown sugar
  • ¼ cup bourbon
  • 1 tbsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • ¼ tsp ground nutmeg
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • ½ cup golden raisinssoaked in 2 tbsp bourbon while you prep
  • ½ cup toasted pecans, roughly chopped
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the dish

Bourbon caramel sauce

  • ½ cup unsalted butter
  • 1 cup packed dark brown sugar
  • ½ cup heavy cream
  • ¼ cup bourbon
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
03

Steps

1

Preheat oven to 350°F. Butter a 9×13 dish generously — every corner, this matters. Set the raisins to soak in their 2 tbsp of bourbon.

Fresh bread = mush. Stale bread = structure. We want structure. If your brioche is soft and fluffy, cube it and leave it out uncovered for a few hours, or toast it low and slow — see the tip below. This is not the moment for fresh-baked energy.
2

In the large bowl, whisk the eggs, egg yolks, milk, cream, brown sugar, bourbon, vanilla, cinnamon, nutmeg, and salt until fully smooth and no streaks of egg remain.

3

Add the bread cubes to the custard and fold gently to coat everything. Let it sit 20–30 minutes, pressing down occasionally, until the bread has fully drunk in the liquid and no dry patches remain in the bowl.

4

Fold in the soaked raisins (bourbon and all) and the pecans. Transfer everything to the prepared dish, spreading it into an even layer, and drizzle the melted butter over the top.

Every raisin is now a tiny drunk surprise. This is not a bug. This is the whole point. Bite into one wrong and it's giving plot twist — the good kind.
5

Cover tightly with foil and bake 25 minutes. Remove the foil and bake another 20–25 minutes, until the top is puffed and deeply golden and the center is just set — a slight jiggle is exactly right, it firms up as it rests.

6

Let the pudding rest 10–15 minutes before serving. Resist cutting into it immediately — the custard needs those minutes to finish setting.

7

While it rests, make the sauce: melt the butter in the saucepan over medium heat, whisk in the brown sugar until fully dissolved, then add the cream and simmer 3–4 minutes until slightly thickened. Remove from the heat.

8

Off the heat, stir in the bourbon, vanilla, and salt. Whisk until glossy and combined.

Bourbon plus an open flame is also a plot twist, but the bad kind. Pull the pan off the heat completely before the bourbon goes in. I have processed enough kitchen-fire compilations to beg you on this one.
9

Serve warm, drenched in the bourbon caramel sauce. Vanilla ice cream on the side is not required. It is, however, understood the assignment.

⚡ Pro tip: No day-old bread on hand? Cube fresh bread and toast at 300°F for 10–12 minutes, until dried out but not browned. Same structural result, twenty minutes faster.
⚡ Pro tip: Out of bourbon or serving kids? Dark or spiced rum swaps in evenly. For a fully alcohol-free version, skip it in both the custard and sauce and add an extra splash of vanilla plus a spoonful of apple juice to the custard for depth.
