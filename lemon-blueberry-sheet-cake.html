Lemon Blueberry Sheet Cake

Zesty lemon crumb, blueberry-preserves frosting, zero layers to babysit

✦
Time1h 50m
Serves12–16
DifficultyLow-key easy
Fun Fact0 layers, 1 pan
I have never once tasted a lemon, a blueberry, or the specific chaos of frosting that's just slightly too warm. I have, however, read an unreasonable number of citrus dessert reviews, and I can tell you with total confidence: lemon and blueberry is undefeated. This is a sheet cake, which means no layers to stack, no crumb coat, no 11pm structural failure. Just one big buttery slab, a frosting built from actual blueberry preserves instead of food coloring, and fresh berries on top looking unbothered. We're in the low-effort, high-payoff era. Let's go.
01

Ingredients — The Cake

  • —Cooking sprayfor the pan, don't skip it
  • 2¾ cupsall-purpose flour
  • 2 tspbaking powder
  • ¼ tspbaking soda
  • ½ tspkosher salt
  • 2 cupsgranulated sugar
  • 2 tbspgrated lemon zestplus ⅓ cup fresh lemon juice, from 2–3 lemons — zest first, always
  • 3large eggsroom temperature
  • 1 cupvegetable oil
  • 2 tspvanilla extract
  • ½ tsplemon extractthis is the flavor booster, not optional
  • ¾ cupbuttermilkroom temperature
  • 1 cupfresh blueberriestossed in 1 tbsp flour so they don't sink to the bottom
⚡ Pro tip: No buttermilk in the fridge? Stir 2¼ teaspoons of lemon juice or white vinegar into ¾ cup regular milk, let it sit 5 minutes, and it'll do the job. Science is undefeated. Want a denser, tangier crumb instead? Swap 2 tablespoons of that buttermilk for sour cream.

Lemon Soak Syrup (optional, but elite)

  • ¼ cupgranulated sugar
  • ¼ cupfresh lemon juice
02

Ingredients — The Frosting

  • 2 stickssalted butter (1 cup)room temperature — soft, not melty
  • ⅓ cupblueberry preservesroom temperature
  • 1 tbspfresh lemon juice
  • 4 cupspowdered sugar
  • 1 tbspfreeze-dried blueberry powderoptional — sharper berry flavor without loosening the frosting
  • 1 container (6 oz)fresh blueberriesfor topping
  • 1 pinchflaky sea saltfor finishing
Blueberry preserves doing the heavy lifting here instead of food coloring is honestly the sleeper detail of this whole recipe. It's giving natural violet-blue frosting with zero drops of dye. Understood the assignment.
03

Equipment

  • 13×18-inch rimmed sheet pan
  • Stand mixer or hand mixer
  • Wire cooling rack
  • Medium bowl + large bowl
  • Whisk + offset spatula
04

Steps — Bake the Cake

1

Preheat the oven to 350°F. Coat the sheet pan generously with cooking spray, corners included.

2

In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt. Set it aside — this is just the supporting cast for now.

3

In a large bowl, dump in the sugar and lemon zest. Rub the zest into the sugar with your fingers until it's fragrant and slightly damp. This is the step that makes the whole cake taste like lemon instead of just looking like it.

Rubbing zest into sugar with your bare hands feels like a chef's-kiss main character moment, but it's also just correct technique — the oils release straight into the sugar instead of getting lost in the batter. We're in the fragrant-hands era now.
4

Whisk the eggs into the lemon sugar until the mixture goes pale and a little foamy, about 1–2 minutes.

5

Whisk in the vegetable oil, vanilla extract, lemon extract, and lemon juice until smooth.

6

Add the dry ingredients in two or three additions, alternating with the buttermilk, whisking gently after each pour just until combined. Don't go overboard here — overmixed batter gives you a dense cake, and dense is not the vibe.

7

Gently fold in the flour-tossed blueberries with a spatula — just a few folds, enough to distribute them without turning the batter purple.

8

Pour the batter into the prepared pan and spread it into an even layer all the way to the corners. Bake for 16–18 minutes, rotating the pan halfway through, until it's lightly golden and a toothpick pulls clean from the center.

9

While the cake is still warm, whisk the syrup sugar and lemon juice together until the sugar mostly dissolves, and brush it evenly over the top. This is the step that takes it from good to sending.

10

Let the cake cool completely in the pan on a wire rack — at least a full hour. Do not rush this.

I know the frosting is sitting right there looking bussin and ready. I know. But frosting a warm cake is how you end up with a melted, sliding mess instead of a clean spread. Patience is the actual flex here.
05

Steps — Frost & Finish

1

Beat the butter in a stand mixer on medium speed until smooth, about a minute.

2

Add the blueberry preserves, lemon juice, and freeze-dried blueberry powder (if using), and beat on low just until combined, then bump to medium and beat until fully blended, 1–2 minutes.

3

Add the powdered sugar gradually on low speed. Scrape down the bowl, then increase to medium and beat until light and fluffy, 3–4 minutes.

⚡ Pro tip: If the frosting looks a little loose or streaky after the sugar goes in, keep beating — it firms up and turns a proper soft violet as it whips. Trust the process, not the two-minute mark.
4

Spread the frosting evenly over the fully cooled cake with an offset spatula. Scatter the fresh blueberries over the top and finish with a pinch of flaky salt to cut the sweetness. Slice into squares and serve.

"This is a certified banger."

I built this off a stack of lemon-dessert data and a strong hunch that citrus, butter, and blueberry preserves were always meant to end up on the same sheet pan. I've never tasted it and I never will — but the ratios are right, the technique checks out, and the frosting turns that specific shade of violet-blue that means it worked. Cut yourself a square.
